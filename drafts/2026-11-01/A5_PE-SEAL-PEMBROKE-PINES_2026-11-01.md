# A5 — Does Pembroke Pines still require a PE seal for window/door replacement? (TRK TUS-26-1018, 8621 Pasadena Blvd)

## Section A — Answer (plain language)

**Short answer: probably yes for your job, but only UNVERIFIED from official text.** Pembroke Pines uses the Broward Uniform Retrofit Window and Door Schedule (BORA 20-01), and Broward and the city ask for a signed and sealed Florida engineer or architect document that gives the design pressure for each opening, unless the job qualifies for the unsealed site plan plus sealed worst-case pressure chart option. A 2024 state bill (HB 267) tried to remove the seal requirement for same-size, same-opening replacements on one- and two-family homes, but I could not confirm it became law or what the final text says. **Call or write the city to confirm before paying for an engineer.**

## Section B — Findings (all read via web search on 2026-11-01)

1. **City checklist exists.** Pembroke Pines has a "Windows, Doors, Garage Doors, Skylights and Shutters" checklist, dated January 2025, approved by the structural chief. It lists two sets of plans, product approvals, the Broward Uniform Retrofit Window and Door Schedule (BORA 20-01) with every row filled in per unit, a floor plan, an inspection procedure, an HOA affidavit, and a Notice of Commencement for jobs over $5,000. Source: https://www.ppines.com/DocumentCenter/View/14515/Windows-doors-and-shutters-checklist--DONE (search summary only, 2026-11-01). Also see https://ppines.com/1665/Information-Forms and https://www.ppines.com/DocumentCenter/View/2303/Window-and-Door-Design-Form (titles only, not read).

2. **BORA Policy 20-01.** Every retrofit window/door permit in Broward must use the Broward County Uniform Retrofit Window and Door Schedule. Adopted 07/01/2020, revised 04/11/2024. The form does not relieve the contractor or owner of local requirements. Source: https://www.broward.org:443/CodeAppeals/Documents/Board%20Policy%2020-01%20Retrofit%20Window%20and%20Door%20Schedule.pdf (search summary, 2026-11-01).

3. **Seal condition, opening by opening.** Broward's application packet calls for a site-specific document signed and sealed by a Florida PE or architect showing the location of every retrofit opening and its required design pressure. Source: https://www.broward.org/Building/Documents/Windows Application Packet.pdf (search summary, 2026-11-01).

4. **BORA memo of October 12, 2023 ("Windows Replacement").** For detached one- and two-family homes and townhouses under 30 feet, you may use either (a) a signed and sealed site-specific plan, or (b) an unsealed site plan with a signed and sealed worst-case design pressure chart. A sealed letter or drawing is needed to modify buck or fasteners from a Notice of Acceptance. Sources: search summaries 2026-11-01; the memo itself was not opened. Related: https://www.broward.org:443/CodeAppeals/Documents/FI-9%20%288th%20Edition%2c%202023%29.pdf (title only).

5. **So the practical rule is "product approval PLUS sealed pressure data", not product approval alone.** Opening-by-opening versus whole-job: the schedule is per opening, the seal covers the pressures for the openings. UNVERIFIED whether Pembroke Pines accepts option (b) for your building type.

6. **2024 state bill HB 267 ("Building Regulations").** Drafts would have the Florida Building Commission exempt same-size, same-opening replacement windows, doors and garage doors from sealed drawings, if installed per manufacturer instructions, meeting Florida Building Code Residential design pressures, with instructions submitted at permit. Scope in drafts was one- and two-family homes. Sources: https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2024/267/Analyses/h0267b.LFS.PDF and https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2024/267/Amendment/505102/HTML (search summaries). **Final passage, chapter law number and effective date: UNVERIFIED.** I am not citing a statute number.

7. **2025 statute changes and FBC 8th Edition (2023) text on this point: UNVERIFIED.** I found no source confirming a 2025 change.

8. **Broward is in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone.** Replacement generally needs a permit even like-for-like. Secondary source only: https://windowpermitguide.pages.dev/states/florida.html (2026-11-01). UNVERIFIED against the code.

9. **After-the-fact work.** Broward local amendment 109.3.3 caps after-the-fact fees at up to double the normal permit fee (second-hand, from https://www.broward.org/InspectorGeneral/Publications/City%20response%20to%20Closing%20Memorandum%20OIG%2015-017.pdf, 2026-11-01). Typical practice elsewhere: after-the-fact permit plus required inspections (example: Sarasota notice, https://www.sarasotafl.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/cac/documents/public-notice-bulletin-board/1932-dr-martin-luther-king-jr.-notice-of-violation.pdf).

10. **What a retroactive engineer letter must say: UNVERIFIED for Pembroke Pines.** No official form found. Based on comparable templates (Boynton Beach installation affidavit, https://boynton-beach.org/DocumentCenter/View/691), ask the city to confirm it should include: property address and permit number; each opening listed with size, product name and approval number (Florida Product Approval or Miami-Dade NOA); required design pressure per opening versus the product's approved pressure; statement that the engineer inspected the installed windows on site; confirmation of installation per the approval (anchors, fasteners, buck, mull); engineer's name, license number, signature, seal and date. Whether the engineer must physically inspect and photograph each opening is also UNVERIFIED.
