## Section A: What the program is for

**Find owners of open Miami-Dade Unsafe Structures cases who are not yet being helped, reach them, and keep the rest of the cases as a list of design professionals to meet.**

Every case that is already being worked is a wasted call. So the list must be refreshed constantly, and every case is graded before anyone spends effort on it.

## Section B: Getting the cases

1. Open the county Unsafe Structures report. Pick a report using the filters. The property-type drop-down is the important one: single-family, commercial, industrial. Commercial and industrial are the target.
2. The county only exports to Excel. Convert the Excel file to CSV. CSV is the format Claude reads best.
3. Save the CSV in Drive under a new version name. Never overwrite an earlier pull.

## Section C: How a case climbs through the county

1. It starts as a Notice of Violation (NOV), the lowest level.
2. It moves to other departments and the case number usually changes.
3. Near the end it becomes an Unsafe Structures case, which runs in parallel with the RER lien, still inside Building and Zoning.
4. If nothing is corrected, part of the lien moves to the finance department. That is the "hard lien". It is recorded with the Clerk of Courts and becomes a public record.

The county log shows each step. Importing that log teaches the system how the county escalates over time.

## Section D: Grading each case (the report's second half)

For each case, look in the building department permitting history panel. Use a saved report or build one. Search by the process number.

1. **Any process number that starts with C and is dated after the violation date**, or any permit number, means someone is already working the case. **Do not solicit.** Save it, with the date the number was added, as a future contact list for the architects and engineers involved.
2. **Read the citation image.** Cases are sometimes closed administratively with no work done. Snip the image, mark the case closed, and take it out of the prospect list.
3. **Read the lien amounts**, including the soft lien, so we know the number.
4. **Everything else** stays a prospect.

Because time and actions keep changing, the whole grade must be refreshed before any mailing.

## Section E: Who owns it

1. Individual owner: use the Property Appraiser mailing address.
2. Company or LLC: look it up on Sunbiz. Record every officer with title, the registered agent, status and every mailing address.
3. About half the time a company owns a company. Repeat the lookup until a real person is reached. Save the whole chain and every alternate mailing address as a file.
4. These names go to skip tracing later. Skip tracing is a separate program.

## Section F: The county conversation log

1. Capture the log of county actions and how the case escalates.
2. Whenever the owner or a representative called or visited, log it with a timestamp. Names, emails and phone numbers written in the log are valuable.
3. More than one contact means more than one entry. Together they show how the case is progressing, what worries the owner and what the county told them.
4. Collect the images and join them into one continuous PDF with a viewable link.

## Section G: If we are hired (extended phase)

1. The building department and code enforcement records must agree the owner is in compliance. That makes the county record a document that offsets the original one.
2. Before lien reduction can start, Miami-Dade Finance inspects and confirms the violation was cured. It is redundant, but it clears the way.
3. Our own math: lien face amount from the recording date, plus simple interest per year. **Open question: Jorge said 12 percent today. The Oct 25 runbook says 12.5 percent. Which is right?**
4. Request a payoff by paying a small fee (it used to be $75; confirm the current amount). The county runs its math and mails a reduced payoff letter.
5. **The first offer is the best offer.** Haggling is allowed, but walking away never gets a lower number later. This is knowledge for whoever negotiates. It is not part of the report.
