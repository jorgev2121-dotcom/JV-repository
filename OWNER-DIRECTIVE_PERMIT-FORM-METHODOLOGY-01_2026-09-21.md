## The directive

Establish a protocol by which an expert agent learns the **methodology and logic** of completing
permit applications — not merely how to fill in one form, but the reasoning that governs it.

### 1. Cover every format variant

Permit applications differ by jurisdiction. Team USA Sales already files across at least five that
appear in the record: **Miami-Dade County (RER/DERM)**, **Bal Harbour**, **Bay Harbor Islands**,
**Pembroke Pines** (Broward), and **City of Miami**. The knowledge base must enumerate the
combinations rather than generalise from whichever form was seen most recently.

### 2. Measure the writable space

For every field, record the **character count the box actually holds**, so entered text never
overruns the space. This matters most in free-text fields — Description of Work / Scope of Work and
similar. On the Miami-Dade form (123_01-52 9/25) the Description of Work line accepted
"Balcony concrete / rebar repair; Alt. Level 1" — that is the kind of measurement to capture per
field, per form.

### 3. Find out whether an official manual exists

Jorge does not know whether a published completion manual exists for these applications. Establish
whether one does — county instruction sheets, plan-review checklists, the "Apply For a Building
Permit" pages each municipality publishes — before writing a substitute from scratch.

### 4. Signature authority — agent versus owner

Determine, per jurisdiction and per document, **when an authorized agent may sign and when the
owner's own signature is required regardless of a power of attorney or equivalent.**

This is not academic. Jorge signs as **Owner's Agent | Plans Expeditor | Attorney-In-Fact**, and the
Miami-Dade application's signature block reads *"Signature of Owner or Owner's Agent."* Where an
agent signature is genuinely accepted, an out-of-state owner never has to be chased for a
notarization at all. Where it is not, no POA cures it. **The line between the two is the single
highest-value thing in this directive** — see the live example in TRK-2026-10052.

### 5. Witnesses on notarized documents

Determine **when a notarized document requires witnesses and when it does not**, per document type.
The permit application itself and the Notice of Commencement do not follow the same rule.

**Owner's stated position, to be confirmed, not assumed:** the notary is permitted to serve as one
of the witnesses, provided they are not a family member or otherwise related to the signer. This is
consistent with general Florida practice for most instruments, but it must be verified against the
specific instrument type before it is relied on.

### 6. Field-level notes and hover text

Attach **notes, or hover-over explanations, to the sections of the application** so the logic is
captured at the moment of completion and survives into the next filing — rather than living in the
head of whoever filled the form that day.

### 7. A side note to the plans reviewer

Where compliance turns on any of these variables, **explain it in a short side note accompanying the
submittal**, addressed to the plans reviewer. A reviewer who can see the reasoning does not have to
guess at it, and does not reject for something that was actually handled.
