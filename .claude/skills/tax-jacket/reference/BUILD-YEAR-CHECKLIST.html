Build-Year Red Flags

Read the property's build year and any addition years off the tax jacket, then run this list. Each window tells the inspector one thing to check on site. Every item carries its confidence tag from the verified reference — nothing here is invented.

Rule first

Untouched, permitted, code-at-the-time work stays grandfathered. The moment work is altered or legalized, grandfathering is lost — the after-the-fact permit is judged against current code (or the code when the work was done), at the building official's discretion, usually with a higher fee. So an addition year matters as much as the build year.

Confirmed solid year — cite it. Approx treat the year as a window, not a line. Unverified do not cite without a primary source.

B

The checklist

One row per threshold. Lead with the bolded action; the source note is the why.

Pre-1978Lead-based paint

If built before 1978 → presume lead paint; trigger federal disclosure.

Confirmedthe cleanest threshold in the reference.

Federal residential lead-paint ban, 1978. ~75% of pre-1978 homes still contain some lead; the rule never required removal, only disclosure on sale or lease.

Pre-~1980Asbestos materials

If built or renovated before ~1980 → presume asbestos until tested — floor tile, mastic, popcorn ceiling, pipe wrap, siding, joint compound.

Confirmeda window, not a ban year.

Heavy residential use ~1920–1980. There was never a clean cutoff — say "presume before ~1980," never a single ban year.

1978–1995Polybutylene pipe

If built 1978–1995 → look for gray (sometimes blue or black) plastic supply pipe. Often uninsurable or a forced re-pipe.

Confirmedsay "discontinued / removed from code after 1995," not "banned."

Chlorine in city water degrades poly-B from the inside; fittings crack and burst. Replaced by CPVC and PEX. Production ceased ~1996.

1965–1973Aluminum wiring

If built 1965–1973 → inspect for solid aluminum branch circuits — a named fire hazard, not cosmetic.

Confirmedsome sources stretch the window to ~1975.

Copper-price spike drove single-strand aluminum into ~2 million homes. Connections loosen and arc; pre-1972 homes ran ~55x more likely to reach fire-hazard conditions. Fix: full re-wire or COPALUM crimps.

Pre-1975Cast-iron drain · SoFla

If built before 1975 in South Florida → scope / camera-inspect any original cast-iron drain line. It is past its failure window.

ConfirmedApproxera confirmed; exact failure age varies 25–50 yr.

Standard drain material through the 1970s. Salt-rich soil, humidity and hard water make SoFla lines fail at just 25–30 years — a regional acceleration, not a national number.

~1930–mid-70sGalvanized supply

If built before ~1970 → expect galvanized supply pipe at end of life — low pressure, rusty water, imminent re-pipe.

Confirmedera & lifespan solid.

Standard water-supply material ~1930s–late 1960s; gone from new work by the mid-1970s. Rusts from the inside out. Caution: "black pipe" can mean gas line, not galvanized — confirm which before writing it in a report.

2001–2009Chinese drywall · FL

If built or remodeled 2001–2009 (peak 2005–2008) → check for blackened A/C coils, corroded ground wires, and a sulfur odor.

ConfirmedFlorida was the epicenter — >60% of complaints.

Imported during a drywall shortage; off-gasses sulfur that corrodes copper — worse in hot, humid climates. ~100,000+ homes affected.

FPE 1950–90
Zinsco 1963–81Hazard panels

In these windows → check the panel brand for Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) or Zinsco — a stand-alone fire hazard whatever the build year.

Confirmedpresence alone is the red flag.

Both can fail to trip on overload or short. CPSC: 85% of FPE double-pole breakers failed UL criteria; a 2005 NJ court found FPE fraudulently labeled. Zinsco breakers can fuse to the bus bar. Remedy: replace the whole panel.

1960s +Central duct A/C · SoFla

If built before 1960 in SoFla → any central system is a retrofit — scope the ductwork. Ducts "lying around" or making no sense flag an unpermitted addition.

ConfirmedApproxadoption era confirmed; no single "required by code" year.

By the 1960s–70s central duct A/C was standard in new SoFla homes. Abandoned or illogical runs mean a retrofit or reconfiguration — possibly conditioned space added without a mechanical permit.

Any eraFEMA below-BFE

In a flood zone, any year → look for a step-DOWN into a glassed rear room below the main slab with no flood vents. That is the "sunroom" tell.

Confirmedthe relabel is the recognized dodge, not a statute.

Below-BFE enclosures may only be parking, access or storage, with flood-resistant materials and vents. Finishing one as living space (or blocking vents) violates the floodplain permit — a live NFIP and insurance problem. The name "sunroom" does not change the elevation.

Any eraBathroom clearance

On any added or expanded bath → measure the fixture clearances, not the room size. A toilet under 15 in to center / 21 in in front, or a shower under 30×30, is objective evidence the work would not have passed permit.

Confirmedclearance is the code; there is no minimum room size.

Current clearances: water closet ≥15 in centerline to any wall, ≥21 in clear in front; shower ≥30×30 in (fits a 30-in disc). A crammed vanity beside an enlarged shower is the signature of an unpermitted conversion.

C

Do not cite

The reference flagged these as unverified. They are listed so no one reintroduces them as fact. Verify with a primary source or the building department before any use.

UnverifiedDo not cite without a primary source

The "5′×7′ → 5′×7.5′" bathroom minimum

UNVERIFIED. No source documents this code change. What is supported is the traditional ~5×7 to 5×8 footprint, and that layout is really driven by fixture clearances (above), not a stated minimum. Do not cite the 5×7.5 figure.

A specific "showers required by code" year

UNVERIFIED. Showers became the norm with the post-WWII boom, but no single "required by code" year was found. Do not state a year.

"Code of the year of the infraction"

APPROX — jurisdiction-dependent. The right instinct (altering or legalizing loses grandfathering) holds. But whether an after-the-fact permit is judged against the code at the time of the work or current code is the building official's call — confirm with the relevant building department per job.
