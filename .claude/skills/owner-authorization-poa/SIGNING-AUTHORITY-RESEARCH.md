# Signing Authority Research: Who Can Sign for the Owner (Miami-Dade)

Researched 2026-09-23 by the cloud session (Opus 5.5). Tracking: TRK-2026-9958 (same project as the owner-authorization-poa skill).

## Section B: The short answer

1. **The client is mostly wrong about the permit application.** Florida Building Code 105.1 says "any owner or owner's authorized agent" applies for a permit. Miami-Dade's own pages say "any owner, authorized agent or contractor" may apply. So an agent can sign an ordinary contractor-pulled permit application.
2. **The client is right about two documents:**
   1. **Notice of Commencement.** The statute says "the owner must sign the notice of commencement and no one else may be permitted to sign in his or her stead." Miami-Dade says the NOC must be signed "by you, the owner... and not your agent."
   2. **Owner-builder permits.** The owner must personally appear (in person or virtually) and sign. An agent cannot do this.
3. **Entities and trusts always sign through a person.** For an LLC, corporation or trust, the "owner's" signature is given by the manager, officer or trustee. The NOC form itself lists "Authorized Officer/Director/Partner/Manager", and the notary block lists "officer, trustee, attorney in fact."
4. **Big open question: a power of attorney on the NOC.** The statute says only the owner may sign. But the statutory notary block lists "attorney in fact" as a signing capacity, and 709.2201 says an agent's act counts as the principal's own act. These conflict. **Treat an NOC signed by an attorney-in-fact as risky until the Miami-Dade Clerk or a Florida construction lawyer confirms it.**
5. **A Florida POA only works for what it names specifically.** General "do everything I could do" language grants nothing (709.2201). The POA must name building permits and Notices of Commencement.

## Section C: Document by document

### C1. Miami-Dade building permit application (form 123_01-52)

1. **An owner's authorized agent may apply.** CONFIRMED. Florida Building Code 105.1: "any owner or owner's authorized agent who intends to construct... shall first make application to the building official." Source: https://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/commission/FBC_0415/Commission_Education_POC/701/701-0-MATERIAL.pdf and Miami-Dade BORA memo https://www.miamidade.gov/building/library/memos/2022-04-26-bora-guidance-on-fbc-7-edition-private-providers.pdf
2. **Miami-Dade's form uses the same rule.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). The form says "Any owner, authorized agent or contractor who desires to construct... must first submit an application." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/building-permit.pdf
3. **Signature line wording.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). The signature line on the current form reads "Signature(s) of Owner(s) or Owner(s)' Authorized Officer/Director/Partner/Manager," under an "Owner's/Permit Applicant Affidavit" sworn under penalty of perjury. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/building-permit.pdf
   - Note: this wording is about entity signers. It does not say "Owner's Agent." The "Owner or Owner's Agent" wording the client quoted may be from an older version. UNCONFIRMED which version is current.
   - **Seen directly, 2026-09-23:** the county form actually filled for TRK-2026-1667 (footer "123_01-52 9/25 -- PAGE 1 OF 6", Herbert S. Saffir Permitting and Inspection Center) has its left signature line printed as **"Signature of Owner or Owner's Agent"**, with a Miami-Dade notary block under it. So the 9/25 revision of the form does name the owner's agent. The online PDF above may be a different revision.
4. **The application must be notarized and signed by the property owner.** CONFIRMED. "A completed Building Permit Application (yellow form), notarized and signed by the property owner is required." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/homeowners-general-permit-info.page
5. **Is there a rule that an individual homesteader must sign personally, with no agent allowed, for a contractor-pulled permit?** UNCONFIRMED. No such rule was found. The only "personal" rule found is for owner-builder permits (C3). Items 1 to 4 point the other way.
6. **Whether Miami-Dade accepts a POA attorney-in-fact on the yellow form for an individual.** UNCONFIRMED. No county page was found saying yes or no. Call the Permitting Assistance line (786-315-2000) before relying on it.
7. **Corporations: authorization letters accepted.** CONFIRMED. "Authorization letters are accepted from corporations, but it must clearly state the individual signing is authorized to pull permits on behalf of the owner/company." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/homeowners-general-permit-info.page

### C2. Notice of Commencement (Fla. Stat. 713.13)

1. **The owner must sign.** CONFIRMED. 713.13(1)(g): "The owner must sign the notice of commencement and no one else may be permitted to sign in his or her stead." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/713.13
2. **The statutory signature line allows entity signers.** CONFIRMED. The form reads "(Signature of Owner or Lessee, or Owner's or Lessee's Authorized Officer/Director/Partner/Manager)." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/713.13
3. **Verification.** CONFIRMED. The notice is verified under s. 92.525: "Under penalties of perjury, I declare that I have read the foregoing notice of commencement and that the facts stated therein are true to the best of my knowledge and belief." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/713.13
4. **The notary block names "attorney in fact."** CONFIRMED. "The foregoing instrument was acknowledged before me by means of physical presence or online notarization... by (name of person) as (type of authority, e.g. officer, trustee, attorney in fact) for (name of party on behalf of whom instrument was executed)." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/713.13
5. **Miami-Dade says: owner, not agent.** CONFIRMED. "The NOC must be signed by you, the owner contracting the improvements and not your agent." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/notice-of-commencement.page (same wording on https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/owner-builder-permits.page and https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/contractor-owner-builder-responsibilities.page)
6. **"Owner" means the person holding an interest who contracts for the work.** CONFIRMED. 713.01: "Owner" means a person who owns "any legal or equitable interest in real property, which interest can be sold by legal process, and who enters into a contract for the improvement." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/713.01
7. **Whether a POA attorney-in-fact counts as "the owner" for 713.13(1)(g).** UNCONFIRMED. The statute text points both ways (items 1 and 4). "Not your agent" most clearly bars a contractor or expediter. No court case or Clerk rule was found on POAs. A law firm article discusses contractor-signed NOCs, which is secondary and not relied on: https://www.ansalaw.com/what-if-the-contractor-rather-than-the-owner-signs-a-notice-of-commencement-is-the-notice-defective-and-therefore-ineffective/
8. **Recording and posting.** CONFIRMED. The NOC must be recorded and posted at the job site before the first inspection. Miami-Dade requires recording within 90 days before work starts. Sources: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/713.13 and https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/notice-of-commencement.page

### C3. Owner-builder disclosure statement (Fla. Stat. 489.103(7))

1. **The owner must personally appear and sign.** CONFIRMED. "To qualify for exemption under this subsection, an owner must personally appear and sign the building permit application." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/489.103
2. **The disclosure statement must be signed by the property owner.** CONFIRMED. It "must be completed and signed by the property owner and returned to the local permitting agency" before the permit is issued. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/489.103
3. **Miami-Dade applies it in person or by video.** CONFIRMED. "The owner(s) must appear in person or virtually to affirm that they understand their obligations." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/owner-builder-permits.page
4. **Only individuals can be owner-builders in Miami-Dade.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). "The property name must be in a person's name and not under a company or trust." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/owner-builder-permits.page
5. **Result: an attorney-in-fact or agent cannot sign the owner-builder disclosure.** This follows from items 1 to 3. No POA exception was found. The only statutory exception is for certain electronic solar permits.

### C4. Renewals, extensions, revisions, inspections, cancellation, change of contractor

1. **Application extension.** CONFIRMED. The extension request "must be signed by either the property owner, design professional or contractor of record." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/how-to-apply-for-permit.page
2. **Plan revisions.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). The plan revision form has a "Signature of owner or owner's agent" line, sworn before a notary. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/forms/plan-revision.pdf
3. **Inspection requests.** CONFIRMED. "The permit holder will schedule the inspections online or by calling (786) 315-2100." No owner signature is needed. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/inspections.page
4. **Permit cancellation.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). Send the cancellation form to RERPERCAN@miamidade.gov. If the contractor is unavailable or unwilling, the property owner may make the request. Sources: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/permit-cancellation.pdf and https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/instructions/refunds-permit-cancellations.pdf
5. **Change of contractor.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt).
   1. Before a permit is issued: a new notarized application "signed by the owner/owner's agent and the new contractor."
   2. After a permit is issued: a Hold Harmless letter signed by the owner, notarized, plus a 10-day waiting period.
   Sources: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/hold-harmless.pdf and https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/contractor-change.pdf
6. **Whether "owner's agent" on these forms includes a POA attorney-in-fact.** UNCONFIRMED. It very likely does, since an attorney-in-fact is a formal agent, but no county page says so.

### C5. Code enforcement and unsafe structures

1. **Extension requests.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). A request for more time to comply with a Notice of Violation must be "made in writing by owner or duly authorized representative prior to the expiration of the period allowed for compliance," addressed to the Building Official, under Code of Miami-Dade County s. 8-5(h). Source: https://bldgappl.miamidade.gov/documents2/buildingsupport/novus/1504594.htm
2. **Unsafe Structures Board hearings.** CONFIRMED that the board hears appeals from "homeowners or other interested parties." Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/building/boards-unsafe-structures.asp
3. **Whether an agent may appear at a hearing, and who may sign a compliance agreement.** UNCONFIRMED. Nothing found.
4. **Related zoning practice.** CONFIRMED that zoning has a separate "Ownership Affidavit for Corporation by its Attorney in fact" and an individual's "Power of Attorney for Public Hearing." This shows the county accepts attorneys-in-fact for zoning hearings. It does not prove the same for building matters. Sources: https://www.miamidade.gov/zoning/forms.asp and https://www.miamidade.gov/zoning/library/forms/power-of-attorney.pdf

### C6. Florida power of attorney rules

1. **Signing rules.** CONFIRMED. 709.2105(2): "A power of attorney must be signed by the principal and by two subscribing witnesses and be acknowledged by the principal before a notary public or as otherwise provided in s. 695.03." Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/709.2105
2. **General "do anything" wording grants nothing.** CONFIRMED. 709.2201: general provisions "purporting to give the agent authority to do all acts that the principal can do, are not express grants of specific authority and do not grant any authority." Source: https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/709.2201
3. **An agent's act binds the owner as if the owner did it.** CONFIRMED. 709.2201: the act "has the same effect... as if the principal had performed the act." Source: https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/709.2201
4. **Homestead limit.** CONFIRMED. If the owner is married, the agent may not mortgage or convey homestead without the spouse joining (709.2201). This covers deeds and mortgages, not permits. Source: https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/709.2201
5. **Recording the POA.** CONFIRMED. 709.2106: an original POA "relied upon to affect the title to real property may be required for recording," and may be recorded with the Clerk. Source: https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/709.2106
6. **Is an NOC an instrument that "affects title," so the POA must be recorded with it?** UNCONFIRMED. An NOC is recorded but does not transfer title. The safe practice is to record the POA at the same time. No rule was found saying this is mandatory.
7. **Recording format rules.** CONFIRMED. 695.26: each signer's and witness's name must be printed beneath their signature, with a post-office address, plus the name and address of the person who prepared it. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/695.26
8. **The Miami-Dade Clerk records powers of attorney.** CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/faq.page?Mduid_faq=faq163251709100786

### C7. Entities: resolution, not POA

1. **Corporations: officer plus authorization letter.** CONFIRMED for building permits (see C1 item 7). The letter must name the individual authorized to pull permits.
2. **LLCs: manager or member signs.** CONFIRMED only that 605.04074 (agency of members and managers) and 605.0302 (statement of authority) exist. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0605.04074. The exact wording was not retrieved, so the detail is UNCONFIRMED.
3. **Whether Miami-Dade accepts an LLC resolution, a Sunbiz print-out, or both.** UNCONFIRMED. No county page names a Sunbiz print-out. Treat it as proof of who the managers are, not as authorization.
4. **Trusts: the trustee signs as trustee.** CONFIRMED that 736.1017 lets a trustee give a "certification of trust" instead of the trust document, and third parties may rely on it. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/736.1017. The NOC notary block names "trustee" (C2 item 4).
5. **Trusts cannot be owner-builders in Miami-Dade.** CONFIRMED (C3 item 4).

### C8. Enhanced life estate (Lady Bird deed)

1. **The life tenant controls the property and may improve it without the remaindermen.** UNCONFIRMED from a primary source. Only secondary sources say so. The Florida Bar title standard 6.10 is closest to authoritative: https://www.flprobatelitigation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/837/2023/07/UTC-lady-bird-deed-STANDARD-6.10.pdf
2. **Practical reading:** the life tenant is the "owner" who contracts (713.01 definition, C2 item 6), so the life tenant signs. Remaindermen do not sign. UNCONFIRMED against any Miami-Dade rule.
3. **Owner-builder:** allowed only if the deed is in a person's name (it usually is). UNCONFIRMED for Lady Bird deeds specifically.

1. **Corporate authorization letter.** CONFIRMED (C1 item 7).
2. **Tenant authorization.** CONFIRMED (search excerpt). A letter from the property owner authorizing work is required when a tenant is not authorized to obtain permits under the lease. Source: https://www.miamidade.gov/permits/library/building-permit.pdf
3. **A standard county "Owner's Agent Authorization" form for building permits.** UNCONFIRMED. None was found on the forms list: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/all-forms.page
4. **Cutler Bay** has an "Affidavit Designating Agent for Lien Reduction" and requires the owner's notarized signature or ID for owner-builder permits. CONFIRMED (search excerpt). Sources: https://www.cutlerbay-fl.gov/com-dev/page/building-forms and https://www.cutlerbay-fl.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/community_development/page/4671/owner_builder_disclosure_-_fillable.pdf

## Section D: By owner type

1. **Individual homeowner.**
   1. Permit application with a contractor: the owner signs and notarizes. An authorized agent is allowed by the Building Code. Whether the county accepts a POA holder: UNCONFIRMED, so call first.
   2. NOC: the owner personally. A POA holder is risky.
   3. Owner-builder: the owner personally, in person or by video. No agent.
2. **LLC.** The manager or authorized member signs as the LLC's signer on the application and the NOC. Bring the LLC resolution or authorization letter. It cannot be an owner-builder.
3. **Corporation.** An officer signs. Bring an authorization letter naming the signer. It cannot be an owner-builder.
4. **Trust.** The trustee signs "as trustee." Bring a certification of trust (736.1017). It cannot be an owner-builder.
5. **Lady Bird deed.** The life tenant signs as owner. Remaindermen are not needed. UNCONFIRMED against a primary source.

## Section E: What to tell the client

1. **An authorized agent can sign most permit paperwork.** The Florida Building Code and Miami-Dade's own form say "owner or authorized agent." That covers the application, revisions and change of contractor.
2. **Two things the owner must sign personally:** the Notice of Commencement and any owner-builder paperwork.
3. **An LLC, corporation or trust signs through its manager, officer or trustee,** with a resolution, authorization letter or certification of trust. None of them can be an owner-builder in Miami-Dade.
4. **A power of attorney only works if it names permits and the NOC specifically,** and is signed with two witnesses and a notary. Record it with the Clerk if it will be used on anything recorded.
5. **Using a POA on a Notice of Commencement is still an open question.** Get the owner's own signature on the NOC whenever possible.
