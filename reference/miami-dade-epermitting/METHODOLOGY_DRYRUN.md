# DRYRUN — what it actually is, taught by Jorge Valdes, 2026-09-21

**This is tacit knowledge. It is not published anywhere, and cloud had it wrong.**

---

## Cloud's wrong reading, recorded so the error is not repeated

Seeing `Contractor: DRYRUN` on the Permit Application History screen for process
**C2026170181**, cloud inferred that a dry run was a pre-submittal plan review with no contractor
yet attached, and raised it as a possible *cause* of the reviewer's "contractor information does not
match county records" comment — a structural defect.

**That was wrong, and it had the sign backwards. `DRYRUN` is not a defect. It is a favour.**

---

## What it actually is, in Jorge's words

> *"It was a courtesy extended by the city slash county permit intake reviewer as by changing it to
> dry run, it doesn't hold up the process and would allow it to go to plans review and start moving
> forward. Then I would have three or four days while it was processing to revise the application
> and add the general contractor information. However, after he extended that courtesy, he found
> that smudged seal which stopped the process from continuing on because it's not a valid
> execution."*

### The mechanism

1. An application arrives with the contractor information not yet final.
2. Normally that **holds the application at intake** — nothing moves.
3. The intake reviewer can, as a **discretionary courtesy**, set the Contractor field to `DRYRUN`.
4. That releases the application into **plans review**, so the technical review runs in parallel
   instead of waiting.
5. The applicant then gets a window — **Jorge's figure is three to four days** — to revise the
   application and add the general contractor information properly.
6. The permit still cannot issue until the contractor is attached. `Permit Number: 0` throughout.

### Why it matters commercially

**It buys three or four days of parallel review time.** On a job where the owner is out of state and
signatures take days to collect, that is the difference between a permit moving and a permit
sitting. It is granted by a person, not by the system, which means it depends on the relationship
with the intake desk — and it can presumably be withdrawn the same way.

---

## What actually stopped this permit

**Not the contractor field. The notary seal.**

The reviewer extended the DRYRUN courtesy, the application went forward, **and then the reviewer
found the New Jersey notary seal on a jurat sworn in Florida.** That is not a valid execution, so
the process stopped there.

**Which means Jorge's original instinct — stated before any of the research — was right, and the
three competing theories cloud built were largely noise.** The notary defect was always the blocker.

**This does not make the Qualifier No. question moot.** The contractor information still has to be
added correctly during the window, and `8486` versus the nine-digit field remains a live question.
But it is the *next* step, not the thing that stopped the process.

## ⚠ The timing question nobody has asked yet

Application received **18 September 2026**. The DRYRUN window Jorge describes is **three to four
days**. **Today is 21 September.** That window is at or past its end.

**Open question, and it is urgent:** did the window lapse while the seal problem sat unresolved, or
did the process stopping on the invalid execution pause the clock? If it lapsed, the courtesy may
have to be re-requested from the same reviewer — and that is a phone call, not a form.

**Worth asking on the same call to the intake desk that settles the Qualifier field.**

## The lesson about learning this at all

Jorge's own framing:

> *"Will be interesting to see how much of this I have to teach you and how much you can learn on
> your own."*

**Honest answer: cloud could not have learned this one.** Two research passes ran against the county
and the wider market, and neither surfaced it, because it is not written down anywhere. It is a
discretionary practice between an expediter and an intake reviewer. No statute describes it, no
county instruction sheet mentions it, and the e-Permitting screen shows only the four-letter result
with no explanation.

**That is precisely the argument for the knowledge base in
`OWNER-DIRECTIVE_PERMIT-FORM-METHODOLOGY-01`.** The parts that can be researched — statutes, fee
schedules, form definitions — were found in about three minutes each. The part that actually decides
whether a permit moves lives in one person's head and has to be captured by asking him.

**Rule for every future session: when a county screen shows something unexplained, ask Jorge what it
means before theorising about it.** Cloud built three competing hypotheses on this one and the
correct answer was a single sentence from the person who arranged it.
