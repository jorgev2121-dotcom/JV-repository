## What this proves, and it settles an open question

**2 of 100 threads are governmental. 0 of 100 carry a client job document, a property jacket,
a permit card or a county record set.**

**This is why the property jacket was not in Gmail.** Yesterday I wrote *"zero `bldg jacket`
mail"* and had to correct it — the one real jacket email is in Outlook at
`Jorge@teamusasales.com`, a store this session cannot reach.

**That correction now has a measured explanation rather than an excuse.** The document flow does
not come through Gmail at all. **Twelve forwards from his own work address are the only bridge**,
and a forward is a copy, not a channel.

**Consequence for anything built on Gmail:** an intake watcher, an OCR feed or a jacket catcher
pointed at Gmail will find **two government emails a year.** Any such component must point at
Outlook or it is watching the wrong door.

## Three things worth his eye

**1. `photos@onedrive.com` is the single largest attachment sender in his inbox — 17 of 100.**
Every one is an automated photo-memories email. **Seventeen percent of a year's attachment
traffic is a feature nobody asked for**, and it is noise in every search that filters on
`has:attachment`.

**2. Four QuickBooks invoice threads are addressed to `candy.almonte@iberostar.com`.** Not to
Jorge, not cc'd to him — **addressed to somebody else and landing in his mailbox.** All four
are January 2026. **Worth one look: either an old alias is forwarding, or an address was
mistyped by a sender.** Not urgent, not actioned, flagged only.

**3. Eight `login.gov` emails in two days — 2026-04-06 and 04-08.** That is the signature of a
government identity verification being attempted, possibly more than once.

**Stated carefully, because it touches tonight's Clerk question: `login.gov` is a federal
identity service. It is not the Miami-Dade Clerk.** This does not prove he has a Clerk account
and it must not be reported as if it does. **What it does show is that he has been through a
government registration flow before and it took several attempts** — which is a reason to check
1Password first rather than assume he must register fresh.
