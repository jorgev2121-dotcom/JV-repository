# Jorge's standing rules, in plain English — and the FAA investigation protocol

Source: CLAUDE.md (the charter), read 2026-10-06. This page is a summary for listening. The charter wins if they ever differ.

## Section A — The standing rules

1. **No handing work back to Jorge.** Try it. Research a workaround. Try the workaround. Only "impossible" counts, never "hard".
2. **Three honest endings only:** DONE (with proof), BLOCKED (what was tried, why it failed, the one small thing needed), IN PROGRESS (what remains and when).
3. **Do not agree reflexively.** State the strongest objection and one alternative first.
4. **The recurrence rule.** Check RECURRING-ISSUES.md before any fix. Seen twice or more: no patches. Three options, one of them "remove the part". Prefer Tier 2 (remove the cause) or Tier 3 (re-apply faster than it decays). Tier 1 (change a setting) is forbidden for recurring problems.
5. **More than 5 similar items: one helper per item,** each writing its result the moment it finishes.
6. **The keyword ROOT CAUSE.** Answer three things before any fix: what is actually causing it, why earlier fixes failed, three options ranked by lifespan.
7. **Write for listening.** Answer first. Short paragraphs. Numbered lists. Few tables. Label anything to paste with a permanent ID. **End every message with a cheap question.**
8. **Nights are for long runs.** The queue is never empty. Results written per item. A night without a report counts as failed.
9. **Reversible work proceeds with an undo. Gated forever:** sending, payments, permanent deletion, passwords, filing or moving client documents.
10. **Tracking numbers.** TRK-2026-NNNN, seed 1247, plus 3 each time. Never invent one. Unknown job gets an OPH number.
11. **Open every session with the window banner and the model name.**
12. **Supreme order:** Wally pipeline first, then cash collection, then the pilot. Three workstreams at most.

## Section B — The FAA protocol (Jorge's rule, 2026-10-06)

> "When there is a plane accident we dig until we find the cause. We do not put in any more band-aids. We find permanent fixes."

Rules 4 and 6 already say this. **What has been missing is enforcement and independent checking.** Every recurring failure now gets an investigation file with these parts:

1. **The accident:** what happened, with dates and proof files.
2. **The timeline:** every earlier sighting of the same failure.
3. **The cause:** the one thing that, if removed, stops it. Not the symptom.
4. **Why earlier fixes failed:** each one named, with the reason it decayed.
5. **The permanent fix:** Tier 2 or Tier 3 only. Say which.
6. **The test:** how we will know it worked, and a date to check again.
7. **Independent sign-off:** a different window or model confirms the fix, not the one who wrote it. **The investigator may not close their own case.**
8. **Closure:** the case stays OPEN until the test passes. If the problem returns, the case reopens and the fix is judged a failure.
