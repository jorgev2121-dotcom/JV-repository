### What is checkable the moment the license copy and the comment are side by side

1. **License number.** Form states Contractor No. **CGC1528486**. Must match the DBPR record exactly.
2. **Qualifier / business pairing.** Form pairs qualifier **Miguel Zaldivar** with contractor name

4. **Scope.** CGC is a Certified General Contractor — unlimited scope for building work. Balcony
   concrete and rebar repair at $11,500, Alteration Level 1, sits comfortably inside it. **If the
   county's objection is scope-based, the county is wrong and the license copy rebuts it directly.**

### Where the county may well be RIGHT — read this before drafting any rebuttal

The form has a field reading **"Last four (4) digits of Qualifier No."**, filled in as **8486** —
which is simply the last four of the state license number CGC15284**86**.

On Miami-Dade forms the *Qualifier No.* is generally the **county** qualifier / certificate-of-
competency record, which is a different number from the state DBPR license. If whoever completed
this form copied the last four of the state license into a field asking for the county qualifier
number, **intake would be correct to bounce it**, and the license copy will not rebut anything —
it would confirm the objection.

**I am flagging this as a hypothesis built from the form itself, not as a finding.** I cannot
confirm it without the county's actual wording. But it is the most likely way a "contractor
license" comment on this specific application turns out to be justified, and you should know the
downside case before you write to Miguel asserting the county made a mistake.

Jorge's own license PDF carries the renewal date **in its filename**: *"Renewal date August 31 2026."*

This is still not proof. The filename gives the renewal date, not the renewal *status*. But it means
the license copy must be checked for a current expiration before any rebuttal is written — and it
makes a rebuttal considerably less likely to be the right move.
