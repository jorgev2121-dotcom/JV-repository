## 2. MATH (simple interest on a flat $46,000; then 36-month amortized)

- Annual interest = P × r
- Monthly interest = P × r ÷ 12
- Payment = P × i ÷ (1 − (1+i)^−36), where i = r ÷ 12

## 3. RED FLAGS

### 3A. The "3-4% offers" (no offer document exists to grade)

1. **Too good for the debt type.** A 3-4% rate on unsecured or card debt is far below what lenders normally charge. Treat it as unproven until an APR appears in writing.
2. **Rate vs APR confusion.** Insist on the APR and the total finance charge, not the "rate."
3. **Possible fee mistaken for rate.** A 3-4% balance-transfer fee is common.
4. **Teaser or intro rate.** Ask: what is the rate after the intro period, and when does it reset?
5. **Secured or personal guarantee.** A cheap rate often means the home or business assets back it. The Selene-mortgaged homestead must not be pledged for card debt.
6. **Unsolicited contact, pressure deadlines, advance fees.** Any "pay us first to release the loan" is a scam signature. Stop if asked.
7. **No NMLS or state license.** Check before any call.
8. **Phishing look.** Do not click links in offers. Go to the lender's own website or phone number.
9. **Existing SBA and credit history.** The SBA compromise file and high card use make some 3-4% offers unlikely to be real approvals.

### 3B. Risks in the existing debts
