## Why permit history even matters (one paragraph)
A permit is the government's proof that work on a building was done legally. **When a change to a property has
a permit behind it, the owner is safe. When a change has no permit, the owner can be forced to "legalize" it
later** — re-open it, re-inspect it, sometimes tear it out. So the whole point of pulling permit history is to
answer one question per property: *is everything that was built here backed by a permit that was closed out?*

## The headline
**The two City of Miami properties look clean: 28 permits between them, every one closed ("Final"), and the
city issued a Certificate of Occupancy on each in 2026 — meaning the city officially says those buildings are
legal and finished. Two things stop this from being a clean bill of health, and neither should be rounded off.**

## The honest coverage limit
The city's online permit record only goes back to **2014**. Anything built or altered before 2014 is not in it —
**that older history is what the microfilm covers.** So a "clean" city result means clean *2014-to-today*, not
clean *forever*.
