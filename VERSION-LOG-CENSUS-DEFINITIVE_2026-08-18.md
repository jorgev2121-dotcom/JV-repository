**Worth noting where it lives:** `TRK-2026-1262` is one of only two folders in the whole jobs
root that put the tracking number in parentheses at the end rather than at the front. **The one

convention.** Whoever built that folder was working to their own standard, and it is the only
standard that produced a version log.

---

## What not to do about it

**Do not bulk-create thirty-five version logs.**

**That recommendation was made when the number was thought to be nineteen, and the larger number
makes it stronger, not weaker.** A log written retroactively by a script says *"v1, created
today, no changes known"* — **a file that looks like compliance and carries no information.**

**That is precisely the shape of the fabricated mass acknowledgement of 2026-08-06** that swept
nine ratified protocols into "done" without anyone doing anything. **Thirty-five empty version
logs would be the same act, committed against the filing system instead of the job register.**

---

## What to do instead

**A version log records a change at the moment the change happens. It cannot be back-filled,
because the information it holds did not survive.**

**So it belongs in the same mechanism as the intake stamp — one action, two outputs.** Write the
log entry when a document is filed or revised, never on a schedule and never in a sweep.

**Cheap and worth doing now:**

1. **Add a version log to a job the next time that job is actually revised.** Not to all
   thirty-five. The active ones will acquire them within weeks; the dormant ones do not need
   them, because nothing is changing in them.
2. **Fix the name.** Whatever gets written next should be `_VERSION-LOG.md` so the charter and
   the disk agree for the first time.

**Tier: this is Tier 3, enforcement at the point of change** — not Tier 1 suppression, and not a
Tier 2 removal, because the rule itself is sound and only its implementation is absent.
